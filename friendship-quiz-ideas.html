Friendship quiz ideas: make a “How well do you know me?” quiz

A good friendship quiz is not an exam. It is a playful way to revisit shared memories, notice the small things you remember about one another, and start conversations. The best questions are personal enough to feel meaningful but comfortable enough that everyone can answer without revealing something private.

1. Start with five friendly themes

  • Everyday favourites: comfort food, favourite drink, music or movie genres.
  • Shared memories: a memorable outing, a funny moment or a place you visited together.
  • Dreams and plans: a destination you would like to visit or a skill you want to learn.
  • Habits and preferences: early bird or night owl, quiet weekend or busy plans.
  • Lighthearted choices: the snack you would pick for a road trip or the role you take in a group plan.

2. Write questions with one clear meaning

Avoid questions that combine several ideas. “What would I order for a relaxed Friday dinner?” is easier to answer than “What food, place and activity do I like best?” Specific wording makes the result feel fair and reduces arguments about what a question meant.

3. Make four answer choices distinct

For each question, use four plausible but different choices. Avoid two options that mean almost the same thing. Keep answer lengths similar so the correct choice is not obvious just because it is more detailed. If a question has no single correct answer, it may work better in a personality quiz than a scored friendship quiz.

4. Balance easy and challenging questions

Mix a few obvious favourites with details only close friends may remember. If every question is obscure, the quiz can feel discouraging. If every answer is obvious, the score does not tell you much. A useful starting point is to alternate everyday preferences and shared memories.

5. Keep the quiz respectful

Do not ask for passwords, private contact details, health information, secrets, or anything intended to embarrass someone. Avoid questions that test loyalty or pressure a friend to prove affection. Let the quiz stay optional and light.

6. Check your answer key before sharing

Read each question as if you were a player. Confirm that exactly one answer is marked correct and that the other choices are understandable. Preview the share link before sending it to a group chat. Example questions to adapt

  • Which snack would I most likely bring on a long trip?
  • Would I rather spend a free afternoon outdoors or relaxing at home?
  • Which kind of movie do I usually choose first?
  • What is one place I have talked about visiting?
  • What is my favourite way to celebrate a small win?

Use these as inspiration, not assumptions. Replace them with details that are actually true for your friendship.
